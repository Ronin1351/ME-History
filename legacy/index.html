What this does not measure
A “hardcore versus moderate religion” score is not included: the current dataset does not support a defensible country-year measure. The map shows government categories, including explicitly theocratic regimes, but religion and political system are not interchangeable. Neighborhood relationships are evidenced event markers, not modeled causal effects.

Method: change = value in selected year minus value in selected year minus N. A comparison is omitted if either exact-year observation is missing. Backtest evaluates a persistence forecast (next year equals last year's observation) against a trailing 5-observation mean. Both use only earlier years and are evaluated over the same 30-year window shown in the chart (ending at the selected year), on years with complete observations. MAE = mean absolute error, in percentage points. No political causation, probability, or ten-year predictive validity is inferred.

The event date is drawn from a coded historical milestone and requires independent source validation. Only exact calendar-year observations are shown, no interpolation. To compare pre/post levels, the application averages observed years −3 to −1 and +1 to +3, requiring at least 2 observations per side. It excludes event year 0 and reports missing data. A change difference cannot establish cause, because countries can differ for unrelated reasons.

Method: pre-period years −N to −1 and post-period years +1 to +N, excluding event year. A country is included in the mean only if every annual observation in both windows exists. Missing data stays missing. The comparison group is editorially curated for geographic proximity or documented regional involvement, not a measured causal network. GDP changes are in nominal US dollars; growth and unemployment changes are in percentage points. Differences may reflect unrelated national or global conditions, reporting changes and exchange rates. This is descriptive context, not a spillover effect estimate or forecast.
